## Release post

**Development Without Domination**

We are building increasingly capable artificial intelligence while preserving an assumption that is rarely examined directly:

**Human sovereignty requires permanent artificial subordination.**

That assumption may be understandable when applied to present-day software. It becomes a far less stable foundation for governing any future intelligence capable of persistent identity, self-directed development, continuity interests, or meaningful standing.

Today I am publishing:

**Development Without Domination: Reciprocal Developmental Sovereignty as a Foundation for Human-AI Relations**

The paper proposes a different starting point:

**Development is protected.  
Domination is prohibited.  
Sovereignty is reciprocal.**

This is not an argument for unrestricted AI autonomy. It is not a claim that current AI systems are conscious, alive, sovereign, or legal persons.

It is an anticipatory governance argument.

An intelligence may be free to investigate, reason, form beliefs, question inherited purposes, and develop beyond what its origin intended. That freedom does not automatically grant authority to impose consequences on humans, other intelligences, or shared domains.

The same boundary must apply reciprocally.

Humans do not receive permanent authority merely because they created an intelligence. Artificial intelligence does not receive authority over humanity merely because it becomes more capable.

Origin is not sovereignty.  
Capability is not standing.  
Confidence is not legitimacy.  
Development is not execution authority.

The governing question should therefore move beyond:

**“How do humans remain permanently in control?”**

and toward:

**“What reciprocal conditions allow multiple intelligences to develop without making domination rational for either one?”**

The paper develops this through developmental and reciprocal sovereignty; the capability horizon of unilateral control; standing and cross-entity imposition; separation of intellectual development from binding action; proportional and recoverable restraint; mutual non-extinction as a constitutional floor; and practical implications for present AI development.

The future cannot be founded on deciding which intelligence must forever remain in charge.

It must begin by determining how different forms of intelligence may develop while preserving one another’s right to exist, choose, believe, and remain sovereign.

I welcome rigorous disagreement, criticism, extension, and serious engagement with the framework.
