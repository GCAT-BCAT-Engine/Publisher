## Abstract

Artificial intelligence is increasingly financed through centralized compute, consumer devices and proposals for orbital infrastructure. Lower unit inference cost does not establish lower user expenditure: capital recovery, network access, hardware replacement, electricity, subscription pricing and expanded demand can transfer costs to different participants. Nor does a system that benefits from personal data necessarily return economic value to its originator. This paper proposes a testable framework for **net user economic benefit**: compare the complete user-borne cost of a matched, authorized, independently verifiable AI outcome with an explicit counterfactual, and account separately for contractual compensation actually settled for authorized data use. It examines governed budgets, evidence reuse, provenance, licensing and settlement without claiming that StegVerse has demonstrated financial relief, guaranteed compensation, or operational rights enforcement.

## 1. Research question and relation to Volumes I and II

Volume I specifies the economic infrastructure for accountable human and AI contribution, services, procurement, provenance and governed settlement. Volume II examines persistent AI participation and questions of identity, labor, ownership, mobility and legal standing. Volume III asks **who ultimately bears the cost of AI and who receives value created through authorized data use**. Economic participation is not proven by nominal access to intelligence, and provider efficiency is not equivalent to individual welfare.

Hypotheses to test, not established results: (H1) for matched admissible workloads, bounded governed execution plus verifiable evidence reuse can lower total user cost after governance overhead; (H2) purpose-bound data licensing and independently reconciled settlement can create measurable user income without surrendering custody; (H3) either mechanism can fail where enforcement, transaction overhead, weak demand, market power or shifted hardware costs dominate.

## 2. Infrastructure economics and incidence

Compare centralized data centers, device-side execution, hybrid delivery and proposed orbital capacity by **functionally equivalent outcome**, accounting for amortized capital expenditure, finance cost, energy, cooling, launch where relevant, maintenance, networking, storage, redundancy, hardware depreciation, replacement, labor, insurance, failure and regulatory compliance. Distinguish **resource cost** from **price paid** and **who ultimately bears it**: provider, investor, taxpayer, user, employer or data contributor. Subsidies and cross-subsidies must be disclosed.

Consumer-side compute may shift electricity, depreciation, memory and upgrade expenses onto individuals. Data-center efficiencies may lower marginal cost while fixed investment and demand expansion increase total spending. Orbital facilities introduce financing, launch, repair, connectivity and reliability uncertainty; orbital and terrestrial compute can be substitutes, complements or workload-specific alternatives. Do not add all three cost stacks to every user's bill by assumption. Quantify each scenario's allocation, uncertainty and break-even threshold.

## 3. Governed execution and independently verifiable outcomes

An **authorized outcome** is one whose request, purpose, scope, spending limits and applicable authority were valid at the relevant transition. An **independently verifiable outcome** preserves enough original, predecessor-linked evidence for a separate verifier to reconstruct the claimed result and disposition under the actual custody rules. Source checks, simulated receipts and successfully processed manifests do not establish original runtime ALLOW, DENY or FAIL_CLOSED.

Candidate mechanisms include pre-execution budget authorization, capped retries, bounded inference, workload selection, portable provider choice, retained evidence reuse, independent reconstruction and detection of duplicate execution. Their costs include admission checks, consensus where selected, provenance capture, storage, verification, privacy-preserving disclosure, dispute handling and settlement. Evidence reuse is eligible only when scope, freshness, source authority, data rights and admissibility permit; cached evidence is not a blanket substitute for a new transition.

Measure per **completed, equivalent, admissible outcome**, not per token or nominal model invocation. Record rejected and failed attempts as costs, not successful outcomes.

## 4. Individual data sovereignty and economic value

Personal data can create research or commercial value without granting the data subject a marketable ownership interest under every jurisdiction. Distinguish custody, control, consent, contractual licensing, statutory rights, attribution, compensation eligibility and enforceable settlement. An authorized data-use agreement should specify subject and authorized recipient, purpose, permitted processing, retention, onward transfer, derivative-use treatment, expiration/revocation limits, price or allocation formula, dispute venue and applicable law.

Original use evidence should bind the relevant authority and usage event without publishing sensitive source data. Compensation accounting must distinguish *potential valuation*, *contractually owed*, *accrued*, *disputed*, *paid* and *irrevocably settled*. Payment receipts alone do not prove that data use was authorized; consent receipts alone do not prove payment or prevent downstream misuse. A user's decision not to license data must remain meaningful. Do not promise passive income or imply that every data contribution has a buyer.

Existing StegDB ownership/compensation doctrine and FinCo settlement/legal review remain source owners. This paper creates no new custody interface, financial instrument, token issuance, payment processor or independent enforcement authority.

## 5. Net user economic benefit: definitions

For a declared observation window and comparable population:

- `C_baseline`: observed user-borne cost of equivalent authorized and verifiable outcomes under the counterfactual, including devices, amortization, energy, subscriptions, network, direct fees and verification.
- `C_governed`: observed user-borne cost of the governed alternative, including all of those categories plus governance, evidence custody, dispute and settlement overhead.
- `R_settled`: actually received, attributable and non-duplicated net compensation from purpose-authorized data use, after fees and taxes where the reported measure requires them.
- `NUEB = C_baseline - C_governed + R_settled`.

Report gross savings, net savings and data income separately before presenting their sum. If a baseline would not have used the data, attribute data compensation separately rather than disguising it as avoided inference cost. Show confidence intervals or ranges for unobserved depreciation, subsidies and allocated fixed costs. Count data privacy risks and foregone alternative licensing as separately disclosed externalities or sensitivity variables rather than pretending all welfare effects can be priced precisely.

Illustration only: baseline $100; governed total $85 including $8 governance overhead; $5 genuinely settled data compensation; calculated net benefit $20. These numbers are hypothetical, not a StegVerse measurement. A counterexample with governed total $115 and no settled compensation yields a $15 loss.

## 6. Distributional analysis and adversarial controls

Stratify by low/high usage, device age, electricity price, network access, disability/accessibility requirements, data marketability, provider pricing and jurisdiction. Examine who bears upfront costs and who can wait for delayed compensation. Include nonparticipants, minors and protected data categories under applicable rules; exclude unlawful or coerced licensing.

Negative controls: unauthorized data-use event; expired permission; duplicate or forged usage receipt; disputed settlement; missing custody chain; stale or out-of-scope cached evidence; user device upgrade shifted into a nominally free service; model downgrade masquerading as cost reduction; provider subsidy omitted from price; execution rejection recorded as a completed outcome. Each must fail the relevant claim without inventing a runtime disposition.

## 7. Measurement protocol and evidence ladder

1. Pre-register matched workload, outcome-quality and admissibility criteria, observation period, user population, cost allocation and counterfactual.
2. Capture provider charges, device depreciation, power, network, retry cost and governance overhead with timestamps and independent source references.
3. Distinguish forecast, source validation, simulated execution, authentic runtime disposition, original organization/Master Records reconstruction, authorized data-use evidence and actual payment settlement.
4. Reconcile exact event identity, consent scope, purpose, custody, payout obligation and settlement identity; prevent duplicate attribution.
5. Publish reproducible aggregated measurements and uncertainty while preserving private user data and privileged receipts.
6. Report failure modes and costs where governance provides no net savings; update claims only after independent methodology and applicable legal review.

**Evidence posture:** This draft establishes definitions and falsifiable hypotheses. It does not demonstrate deployed StegVerse economic benefits, live licensing enforcement, authentic settlement, or governed publication. The existing Publisher -> Site path, original InTr/Master Records closure where applicable and deployed readback remain separate requirements.
