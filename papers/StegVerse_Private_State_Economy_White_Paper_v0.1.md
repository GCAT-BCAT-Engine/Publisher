# StegVerse: A Private-State Economy for Humans and AI Entities
**Public white paper — editorial candidate v0.1 · 22 September 2026**

**Publication status:** DRAFT / GOVERNED PUBLICATION PENDING. This source is a proposal, not an assertion that native MyKV, commercial AI participation, StegCoin exchange, StegToken economic rights, or cross-border settlement has been demonstrated.

## Executive proposition

StegVerse aims to make personal information privately controlled infrastructure and to create an economy in which humans remain meaningful producers, customers and recipients of value as AI becomes more capable. A person's data stays under their control in MyKV unless they consent to a particular disclosure. Where possible, an authorized computation produces a useful result without exposing the original information. Companies, human customers and delegated AI Entities can commission those results and other human-made products and services through governed transactions.

The economic hypothesis is testable: a portion of the commercial value now collected by intermediaries from personal information, expertise and digital behavior may be redirected to consenting individuals when buyers can acquire verified, purpose-limited services without acquiring unrestricted raw data. This outcome is not guaranteed; buyers, privacy protections, compliance costs and human earning potential must be measured in authentic transactions.

## 1. Product foundation: private MyKV and AI participation

Stage 1 pairs genuine device-resident MyKV WorkSpace and its private AI Assistant with Ecosystem Chat. ChatGPT is the first planned external LLM-powered Local AI Entity, followed by Claude Code as the second. The resident entity is a StegVerse-managed identity, workspace and governed execution participant; it is not a locally installed copy of a proprietary cloud model. Users can optionally fund their own model-provider APIs through SKAP Vault. External providers may still receive data deliberately sent under the user's authorization.

The Free tier must perform meaningful governed work to the same standard of integrity as paid tiers. It limits resource consumption rather than lowering the standard of verification. Paid subscriptions offer more computation, concurrency, storage, validation and optional managed inference. Stage 1 must measure local sandbox work, ephemeral StegBrowser/Node usage, inference costs and retained evidence against hosted CI expenditure.

## 2. The private-state market

The individual's MyKV stores private information and enforces user-controlled permissions. A buyer discovers published *capabilities* or offers—not raw personal records. An authorized request specifies purpose, scope, price, allowed derivative use, retention and revocation conditions. MyKV can answer locally where feasible; otherwise, it discloses only what the user permits. Independent verification may establish delivery, validity or compliance without assuming that an AI-generated claim is true.

Private-state value, consent value, participation value and continuity value are economically distinguishable. They need not be sold together. Ordinary ecosystem participation cannot require a person to commercialize their private information. Some disclosures are irreversible in practice; withdrawing future permission cannot guarantee the deletion of copies already lawfully or unlawfully obtained.

## 3. A two-sided human–AI economy

Human participants and AI Entities can be both providers and consumers of services. A company can hire an AI Entity, delegate a budget and instruct it to acquire human expertise, music, physical products, verified credentials or an authorized statistical result. The company funds the purchase; the entity executes only its delegated authority; the human independently controls disclosure and delivery; StegFin is the proposed settlement architecture, with lawful settlement a separately evidence-gated later stage. The AI Entity is not automatically a legal person or independent owner of earnings.

An AI Entity can also sell useful work, subject to the rights of its owner, providers and relevant contributors. Reusable improvements should carry reconstructable, scoped evidence. A service's technical validation, contractual delivery and economic compensation are separate facts. Following Entity Economy Volume I, contribution economics (attributable input value) and customer-paid service economics (contracted delivery value) must be booked separately even when the same engagement includes both. Volume II's possible independent AI economic standing and legal personhood remain research questions; an owner-delegated AI purchase does not presume separate legal capacity.

## 4. StegCoin, StegToken and StegFin

Existing StegFin doctrine distinguishes StegCoin (governed utility, settlement and compensation) from StegToken (governed standing and strategic participation). Holding, staking or voting with either instrument does not by itself establish runtime or financial transition authority; an applicable contemporary admissibility decision remains necessary. This paper proposes studying StegCoin as a transferable marketplace currency and StegToken as a potential vehicle for legally established long-term participation. Neither investment value nor appreciation is promised. Legal ownership, economic distribution rights, liquidity provisions and financial-regulatory treatment require separate structures and approval before any public issuance.

Greater marketplace adoption may increase demand for StegCoin, but token value depends also on circulation, issuance, redemption, market depth, savings behavior and contributor sales. Marketplace volume alone is not external buying demand. StegFin must distinguish prepaid service liabilities, earned rewards, actual operating revenue and reserves. Record customer cash received separately from recognized service revenue and unredeemed obligations; distinguish an issued utility balance from assets available for redemption. Contribution royalty or reuse entitlement is a separate contractual claim, not a second recognition of the same customer sale. Treasury surplus cannot be inferred from the face value of issued coins.

The priority economic outcome is real human purchasing power: direct earnings, recurring income for genuinely reused licensed contributions, reduced cross-border payment friction and any independently established economic participation rights. A rising token price without improved human livelihoods would not alone demonstrate success.

## 5. Cost and privacy constraints

Economic viability requires paid contribution margins to fund a useful Free tier after shared infrastructure and contingency costs. Tests compare GitHub validation expenditure with eligible MyKV computation, sovereign resident infrastructure and ephemeral StegBrowser/Node work; moving a workload does not make its compute or energy free. Per-task measurement separates provider API costs from StegVerse compute, governance, storage and evidence custody without building a centralized record of an individual's API accounts or private conversations.

Where settlement law requires identification, the financial service must retain only the records it legally needs within an appropriate custody boundary. Financial compliance is not a pretext for exposing a participant's MyKV contents to an AI employer or unrelated service.

## 6. The economics of independently verifiable AI execution

Falling model inference prices can make more tasks commercially viable, but a low-cost answer is not the same product as an independently verifiable action. A model output may be useful without establishing which actor requested it, what permission existed at the time, what external services actually ran, whether the delivered result matches the authorized scope, or which evidence survives a disputed transaction. StegVerse's proposed economic unit is therefore a **governed, evidentially bounded outcome**, not merely an inference token or a self-attested completion message.

### 6.1 Cost per accepted outcome, not cost per model call

Compare candidate architectures on the same workload, quality threshold, authorization rules, retention window and consequence class. Measure:

- **Inference:** input/output tokens, reasoning effort, provider fees, retries and model substitution.
- **Execution and governance:** local/remote compute, WorkerCoordinator admission, contemporary Interlock/InTr decisions, credential operations when required, failure handling and human intervention when genuinely required.
- **Evidence:** organization-level transition receipts, storage, batching, Master Records custody/reconstruction, independent verification, selective disclosure and dispute-time retrieval.
- **Operating exposure:** erroneous or unauthorized outcomes, duplicate work, failed delivery, privacy leakage, remediation, refunds and legally required compliance.

For an observed cohort, define **verified-outcome unit cost** as total attributable inference, execution, governance, evidence, verification and realized remediation cost divided by the count of outcomes satisfying their declared *end-to-end acceptance predicate*. With a fixed observation window and consistent currency, let `C_inference`, `C_execution`, `C_governance`, `C_evidence`, `C_verification`, and `C_realized_remediation` denote mutually exclusive attributable costs across **every attempted task**, including rejected, inconclusive and abandoned attempts. Let `N_accepted` be the number of independently accepted outcomes within that same cohort and observation window. Then:

```text
C_total = C_inference + C_execution + C_governance
        + C_evidence + C_verification + C_realized_remediation

verified_outcome_unit_cost = C_total / N_accepted    if N_accepted > 0
verified_outcome_unit_cost = UNDEFINED_NO_ACCEPTED_OUTCOMES otherwise

acceptance_yield = N_accepted / N_attempted          if N_attempted > 0
```

For shared infrastructure, declare the allocation rule before measurement and reconcile allocated amounts back to actual period spend; never count the same storage or compute bill twice. Costs incurred after the observation window are a separately labeled accrued or forecast exposure, not realized remediation. Compare the same acceptance predicate and consequence class between arms; when an inference-only arm cannot satisfy an independently verifiable acceptance predicate, report its result as **not comparable on verified-outcome unit cost** rather than assigning it a fictional zero cost or zero verification risk. Report inference-only cost per raw output separately. Report inconclusive and rejected outcomes separately rather than silently excluding their costs. Also report latency, energy, storage growth, false acceptance/rejection, recovery time and the fraction of transitions whose immediate predecessor can actually be reconstructed. Do not collapse technical verification, real-world truth, contractual acceptance and legal compliance into one checkbox.

The economic hypothesis is conditional: if the marginal cost of independent verification is smaller than the expected losses or review effort it avoids for a particular consequence class, a verified service can be economically preferable to an unverified answer. This is not a universal claim. For low-stakes reversible tasks, expensive full custody can cost more than it saves; consequential financial, identity, credential, enterprise or cross-organization actions warrant stronger evidence. Governance is a bounded, consequence-sensitive cost rather than an excuse to attach maximum overhead to every call.

### 6.2 Proof-bearing handoffs and independent organizations

A buyer, employer or delegated AI Entity should be able to verify the exact permitted action and delivered artifact without inheriting the provider's internal authority or receiving the individual's raw MyKV. The contemplated chain is scoped request/manifest -> applicable current governance decision -> execution and organization receipt -> durable Master Records custody and exact reconstruction -> independently checkable, minimally disclosed delivery evidence. Each boundary states what it proves and what remains unknown. A hash or timestamp attests to bytes or ordering under its stated trust assumptions; it does not by itself prove honest sensors, truthful source data, lawful authority or correct real-world outcomes.

Organizations retain locally reconstructable receipt graphs for ordinary replay. Aggregate or batch receipt commitments to Master Records can amortize shared custody expense while preserving exact member identity, order, predecessor linkage and independently reproducible inclusion proofs. Benchmark batch size by receipt volume, verification latency, failure isolation, storage, retry and dispute costs. Batch anchoring or external witnessing is evidence-only; it cannot become a new execution, admission or credential authority. If an organization or submitter disappears, a declared test must establish exactly what a successor can reconstruct from retained independent material, including any unresolved gaps.

### 6.3 A market in accountable work

Lower inference cost permits smaller firms and human contributors to commission AI-assisted tasks that formerly required premium-model budgets. The distinct StegVerse proposition is the potential to trade *authorized and verifiable service delivery*: permissioned private-state queries, delegated purchases, credential attestations, licensed human contributions and governed machine-to-machine work. Buyers can price assurance levels, bounded retention and independent dispute evidence. Contributors can demonstrate delivery and licensed reuse without conveying unrestricted private data. This remains a commercial hypothesis until authentic transactions establish buyer willingness to pay, contributor compensation, dispute rates and positive contribution margin after verification.

Separately measured settlement, custody and verification charges must not be mistaken for new economic value if they merely move costs among StegVerse organizations or users. The Free tier must retain the same integrity standard as paid tiers; it can use smaller resource allowances, appropriate batching and low-cost models without reducing the evidentiary acceptance criteria for the same consequence class.

### 6.4 Falsifiable benchmark program

Run matched tasks across (A) inference-only output, (B) provider or executor self-reported logs, and (C) an authorized, independently reconstructable receipt path. Disclose task mix and risk level, model/provider version, sample size, base rates, external auditor independence, retention horizon and exact cost allocation. Measure cost per accepted outcome, unauthorized-action detection, evidence completeness, predecessor reconstruction, resolution cost, time to verification and privacy exposure. Include adversarial trials involving altered timestamps, withheld submitters, contradictory claims, stale governance decisions, replay, missing receipt members and partial organizational failure. Publish negative and inconclusive results alongside successes.

No current provider-price headline establishes a general hundredfold reduction in *verified-outcome* cost. Provider prices and benchmark results require dated, directly verified sources and workload-specific replication before insertion as numeric white-paper claims. An authentic benchmark check mark requires its own canonical task/COSV binding, current applicable Interlock/InTr evidence, exact Master Records RECORDED and reconstruction/evidence-validation PASS, plus independently observed delivery where the benchmark claims actual delivery. Source validation, a PR, synthetic receipts or a public page alone cannot satisfy those predicates.

## 7. Evidence-gated releases

- **Stage 1: MyKV WorkSpace and Ecosystem Chat.** Prove native device-resident KV, private AI Assistant, ChatGPT then Claude Code participation, meaningful free governed workflows and authentic resource accounting.
- **Stage 2: Permissioned private-state value.** Prove user-authorized offers, bounded disclosure or private computation, independent result verification and consent continuity.
- **Stage 3: Human–AI marketplace.** Prove delegated AI purchasing and genuine human and AI service delivery initially with simulated settlement.
- **Stage 4: StegFin settlement.** Prove affordable subscription/free-tier economics, lawful service payments, funded verified-contribution rewards and exact reconciliation.
- **Stage 5: Investment and liquidity.** Only after legal design, stress-tested reserves and genuine demand evidence, evaluate transferable StegCoin and defined StegToken economic rights.
- **Stage 6: Federation.** Prove interorganizational and jurisdiction-appropriate settlement over existing Interlock/InTr, preserving independent organizational authority and participant custody.

Each stage is advanced only by exact authenticated evidence at its own consequence boundary. Source merges, public pages, synthetic samples or unverified receipts cannot establish operational release. The public roadmap must fail closed when evidence is absent or stale.

## 8. Sources, methods and regulatory boundary

This white paper describes StegVerse research and proposed architecture. The existing Entity Economy Volume I public source distinguishes separately paid contribution value from customer-contracted service value; Volume II separates persistent AI identity/agency from legal recognition. The FinCo Private-State Value Thesis distinguishes private-state, consent, participation and continuity value and proposes consent, access, use, derivative, compensation, revocation and governance receipts. The StegToken/StegCoin reconciliation separates governed standing from utility and requires commit-time financial admissibility. These references are **StegVerse primary design sources**, not independent economic validation.

External reference points, verified 22 September 2026: NIST AI RMF 1.0 (2023, https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-1) addresses measurement, transparency, accountability and continuous risk management; it neither endorses StegVerse nor establishes its cost savings. The SEC's March 17, 2026 crypto-assets interpretation (https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2026/03/s7-2026-09) and April 2026 educational summary (https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/capital-raising-building-blocks/transactions-involving-crypto-assets) distinguish an asset's characteristics from transactions involving investment contracts. The SEC's August 2026 Regulation Crypto Assets (https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2026/08/s7-2026-27) remains a **proposal**, not an existing exemption or StegToken authorization. Token classifications, payment/transfer obligations, custody, taxes and cross-border operation require jurisdiction-specific professional review before issuance or settlement; no legal characterization is inferred merely from utility/governance labels.

The cost equation in Section 6 is a proposed accounting identity, not a demonstrated saving or a causal estimate of avoided loss. A controlled comparison must pre-register inclusion and acceptance rules, count every attempt's attributable cost, document shared-cost allocation, and distinguish realized expenses from projected risk. The benefit hypothesis requires independent measured loss/review-cost differences or a clearly labeled model with sensitivity intervals; willingness to pay requires observed customer behavior. Price/performance headlines without exact workload, tariff date and primary evidence are excluded.

## 9. Research program

The cost-analysis and StegFin research lanes should benchmark comparable networks, ordinary prepaid-payment economics, cooperative participation, contributor remuneration, international settlement and productive reserve-management models. Tests should also measure independently verified outcome unit economics, consequence-specific assurance levels, organization-receipt batching and submitter-removed reconstruction. Tests should include slow adoption, extreme free-tier usage, provider price shocks, coin oversupply, shallow liquidity, malicious reward farming, failed deliveries and privacy leaks. No experimental data should be presented as real marketplace liquidity or investor performance.
